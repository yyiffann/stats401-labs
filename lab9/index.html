4. Design and Comparison

The choropleth preserves familiar country shapes, locations, and spatial relationships, so it is useful for examining where large economies are located and identifying geographic patterns. However, geographic area remains unchanged, meaning that physically large countries may attract considerable visual attention even though land area is not the variable being shown.

The cartogram changes this relationship by making country area represent GDP. Large economies become much more visually prominent, while smaller economies shrink. This makes differences in total economic magnitude easier to notice, but geographic shape, area, and relative position become distorted. Consequently, some countries are harder to recognize. Linked highlighting helps users connect countries between the two representations.

I use a logarithmic color scale in the choropleth because GDP values are strongly skewed. A linear scale would compress many economies into similar light colors. Overall, color encoding is useful for examining geographic patterns, while area encoding emphasizes differences in economic magnitude. Each representation therefore supports different analytical tasks and involves a trade-off between geographic accuracy and quantitative emphasis.
