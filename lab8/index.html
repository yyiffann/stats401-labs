4. Questions and Findings

1. What are the major semantic topics?

The eight topics are global history and society (225 passages); natural sciences and technology (208); writing, media and research (173); academic rules and student status (165); China and Chinese studies (163); environment, health and policy (155); course credits and degree requirements (143); and university programs and transfer (123).

2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

University programs and transfer appears in 53 formal sections. Academic rules and student status appears in 35 sections, while course credits and degree requirements appears in 20. The matrix shows that these topics extend beyond a single section of the bulletin.

3. Which sections contain the most diverse content?

Among sections containing at least ten passages, Course Descriptions has the highest topic entropy (2.52 bits across 794 passages). Majors follows with 2.46 bits across 136 passages. These sections contain passages from several semantic topics. Course Descriptions is much larger than other sections, so its passage count should not be interpreted as a direct measure of topic diversity.

4. Which passages from different sections are similar?

Passage p00036 in Key Components (PDF page 18) and passage p00075 in Degree Requirements (PDF page 25) are semantic neighbors. Both discuss course credits and requirements for the degree. Although the passages occur in different formal sections, the nearest-neighbor results connect them through their content.

5. Does “credit” occur in one topic or several?

The singular word “credit” appears in 127 passages. Of these, 71 belong to course credits and degree requirements, 35 to university programs and transfer, and 15 to academic rules and student status. A few more occur in other topics. The concept therefore appears in several semantic contexts.

5. Design Description

This explorer uses the official 2021–2022 Duke Kunshan University undergraduate bulletin. PyMuPDF extracts text blocks from its 400 PDF pages, while the PDF outline supplies the chapter, section, and subsection. I excluded front matter, short artifacts, and exact duplicates, and divided long blocks at sentence boundaries. This produced 1,355 passages from 5,376 raw text blocks. Each passage retains its physical PDF page number. The uneven passage counts matter: Course Descriptions alone contributes 794 passages, so the map represents the document’s content density as well as its variety.

I encoded each passage with the normalized all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence embedding. KMeans grouped the original vectors into eight clusters (seed 401). I inspected characteristic TF-IDF terms and representative passages before naming the clusters. UMAP projects the embedding vectors into two dimensions using cosine distance, 15 neighbors, a minimum distance of 0.15, and seed 401. Nearby points suggest related meanings; neither axis has an independent interpretation.

Each dot represents a passage: position shows the projection, color shows its reviewed topic, and radius shows passage length. Search and section/topic menus dim unrelated passages. Pan and zoom help inspect dense neighborhoods. Clicking a passage opens its full text, hierarchy, page, and five nearest passages measured in the original embedding space. The matrix counts passages for each topic and formal section. Clicking a matrix cell highlights its dots; clicking a dot outlines its cell. These links make it easier to see that credit occurs in degree requirements, transfer discussions, and academic rules, even when those passages appear far apart in the bulletin.
