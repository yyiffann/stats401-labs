Temporal Network Observations

  1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days? The network changes from a smaller set of recurring connections in the early days to a denser network later in the period. New relationships begin to appear around Day 21, and many additional connections appear from around Day 41 onward. Overall, the network becomes more connected as more company pairs become active.
  2. Which companies become more commercially active over time? Companies such as Fusion Electronics (c06), Cedar Retail (c03), and Ion Systems (c09) become particularly active as more connections appear later in the 60-day period. The node size shows the total transaction volume for each company on the current day, making these changes in activity visible over time.
  3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters? Yes. During the earlier part of the period, several companies are connected mainly through recurring relationships within smaller groups. As new links appear, especially after Day 41, these groups become more connected to one another and the network becomes less separated.
  4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time? Several new relationships appear during the middle and later parts of the period. For example, connections involving c06 with c08 and c12 appear around Day 21, while additional connections involving c03, c09, c02, c05, and other companies become visible later. Link width represents transaction amount, so thicker links indicate stronger transaction relationships on a given day.
  5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time? More cross-regional connections appear in the later part of the period. For example, later transactions connect companies across Asia, Europe, and North America, including connections such as c03-c08, c03-c11, c09-c01, c09-c06, and c02-c08. The different node border colors make these regional relationships easier to inspect.
